## Proposed scenario: Normandy Short (D-Day to Operation Cobra)

- Start: 6 June 1944, 00:00 UTC (airborne drops from just after midnight; seaborne landings about 06:30; M01).
- End: about 25 to 31 July 1944 (Cobra began 25 July, XIX Corps destroyed the last opposition and the First Army reached Avranches by 31 July; M11). About 55 days, about 1,320 hourly ticks at the engine's 60-minute tick.
- Playable: United States, United Kingdom, Germany (Canada, Poland and Free France would need nation records; the engine currently has no CAN/POL nations: ask lead). Allied ground side is two armies: First US Army (Bradley) and British Second Army (Dempsey) under 21st Army Group (Montgomery) (M01, M11).
- Map: bbox roughly Cotentin/Cherbourg to Caen/Falaise and south to Avranches/Saint-Lô (about 1.9W-0.3E, 48.5N-49.8N). Terrain needs the bocage (see features).

## Command structure (M01, M02, M11)

Allied: Eisenhower (Supreme Commander, SHAEF); Montgomery (21st Army Group, all land forces in the invasion); Bradley (First US Army); Dempsey (British Second Army); Collins (US VII Corps; Utah); Gerow (US V Corps; Omaha, named in M04); Bucknall (British XXX Corps; Gold); Lt-Gen Richard O'Connor (British VIII Corps, Epsom/Goodwood).
German: Hitler; von Rundstedt (OB West, dismissed 1 July, replaced by von Kluge); Rommel (Army Group B, fortifications and coast defence); Dollmann (Seventh Army); Marcks (LXXXIV Corps); Geyr von Schweppenburg (Panzer Group West); von Salmuth (Fifteenth Army, Calais region) (M01, M02).
Dispute (M01): Rommel wanted the panzer reserves close to the coast; Rundstedt/Geyr wanted them concentrated near Paris/Rouen. Reserves for Army Group B included the 2nd, 21st and 116th Panzer Divisions.

## D-Day order of battle, German (sourced where stated)

- Utah/Cotentin: 709th Static Infantry Division (Lt-Gen Karl-Wilhelm von Schlieben), two battalions of the 919th Grenadier Regiment at the beach; 91st Infantry Division (Lt-Gen Wilhelm Falley, killed by a US paratrooper patrol on D-Day) and 6th Fallschirmjager Regiment inland, arrived in May (M03).
- Omaha: 352nd Infantry Division (Lt-Gen Dietrich Kraiss), formed Nov 1943, moved forward in March 1944, about 12,000 men; took under command two battalions of the 726th Grenadier Regiment and the 439th Ost-Battalion. Coast sector 2: 916th Grenadier Regiment; sector 3: rest of 726th; 915th Grenadier Regiment reserve (Kampfgruppe Meyer) south-east of Bayeux. Lost 1,200 men (10 percent) on D-Day (M04, M05, M12). Allied intelligence had identified the relocation on 4 June.
- Gold/Juno/Sword: 716th Infantry Division (static, only about 6,000 men, in the area since March 1942), elements of the 352nd, Ost battalions on the flanks; 21st Panzer Division south-east of Caen, counter-attacked at 16:00 on D-Day between Sword and Juno and nearly reached the coast (M05, M06, M02).
- Armoured reserve (M01, M02): 12th SS Panzer Division Hitlerjugend committed on 7 June around Caen; Panzer Lehr (Panzer Group West reserve, 208 tanks and assault guns on 6 June) committed on 8 June and pushed back at Tilly-sur-Seulles/Villers-Bocage in mid-June (M12); 2nd SS, 1st SS, 9th, 11th, 19th, 116th Panzer had only arrived from the east in March to May 1944 for refit (M01); seven of eleven panzer/panzergrenadier divisions in France were not fully operational (M01).
- 17th SS Panzergrenadier Division, delayed by transport shortages, counter-attacked at Carentan on 13 June (M10). II SS Panzer Corps (9th SS Hohenstaufen, 10th SS Frundsberg) arrived later from the Eastern Front, in time for the June Epsom fighting (M11). 2nd Panzer Division was withdrawn from the Caumont area for the Mortain counter-attack late July (M11).
- Atlantic Wall: best fortifications at Cherbourg and Saint-Malo; Rommel tripled the mines and built beach obstacles and Rommelspargel against airborne landings (M01, M02).

## Ports and supply (sourced)

- Mulberry harbours: two, A at Omaha (Saint-Laurent-sur-Mer, US) and B at Arromanches/Gold (British). Planning requirement: 12,000 tons per day exclusive of motor transport in all weathers (Combined Chiefs, 2 Sep 1943) (M08).
- Mulberry A damaged by the 19 June storm before the pontoons were securely anchored, abandoned after three days; Americans landed men and supplies over the open beaches (M08). Mulberry B was used for ten months, over two million men, four million tons and half a million vehicles (M08).
- Arromanches harbour could receive about 6,000 tons per day, used continuously for ten months; most shipments were over the beaches until Cherbourg was cleared on 16 July (M02).
- PLUTO fuel pipeline from the Isle of Wight to Cherbourg was planned for D+18 but only operational on 22 September (M02): out of scope for the Short.
- Cherbourg: largest port reachable from the landings, 21,000-man garrison under von Schlieben including hastily drafted naval and labour troops (M10).

## Terrain and weather (sourced)

- Bocage (M02 via the bocage article, M11): hedgerow country, fields and sunken lanes between banks topped with tall thick hedges; limits visibility and movement; Cobra aimed to break out of the bocage into Brittany. Confined in France to Normandy, Brittany, Burgundy and parts of the Loire valley. The Goodwood terrain east of Caen is open, dry and suited to armour (M11).
- Weather: storm 19 to 22 June (four days, M01/M08); poor weather in England cancelled bomber support for Epsom (M11); Cobra delayed several times by weather (M11); the Luftwaffe forecast two weeks of storm before D-Day, so German commanders left their posts (M01).
- Air supremacy: Operation Pointblank targeted aircraft production, fuel and airfields before the invasion (M01).
